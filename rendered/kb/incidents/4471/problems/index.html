How each report was placed

  • One report, one id. The export pages overlap, so some reports appear twice. They are counted once.
  • By tenant, not by browser. A report’s cause depends on the tenant: the locale they write dates in and the booking form they run. Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser and is not used here.
  • parser: a day-first tenant whose date came out with day and month swapped. Most bodies name both dates, and each pair is an exact swap.
  • hint: the same swap, but typed into the booking-v3 form, whose hint shows MM/DD/YYYY. People type what the hint says. The parser fix does not reach it.
  • one-day: the date moved by one day, or differs between two people in different cities. That is not a swap. The desk had already noted these reports, but filed them under parser.

Why one ticket hid them

The desk merges on the subject line, not the body (decision 0001). A subject line says how the problem felt (“wrong day”, “dates mixed up”), not what caused it, and most subject lines in #4471 occur under more than one of the problems above. So no subject rule could have kept them apart. Reading the bodies and joining each report to its tenant did.
